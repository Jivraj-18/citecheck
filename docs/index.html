A data story about citations, LLMs and cost

Can an AI model tell whether a citation supports its claim?

I had four AI models check biomedical claims and machine-learning citations. Running them costs per paper, but they flag correct citations per paper, and an editor's time on those costs far more. Counting that time, the best value is for biomedical claims and for machine-learning papers. The catch: they let more wrong citations through than the most cautious models. Papers

False alarms per paper = the model's false-alarm rate × checked citations (the median per arXiv paper here). Missed wrong citations are not priced, because their cost depends on what slips into print.

Where they fail

A screening model can fail two ways: say a wrong citation is fine (a miss), or flag a correct one (a false alarm). On the machine-learning papers almost every failure is a false alarm; the models missed only of the wrong citations. What this does not show

  • How common unsupported citations are. The sample was not designed to measure that.
  • Expert-level verification of the machine-learning examples. Models checked them by reading the full text.
  • That results transfer across fields. SciFact is biomedical; the arXiv set is machine learning.
  • Performance on real mistakes. Swapped citations are easier to catch than the errors authors actually make.
  • A fair sample of all citations. The machine-learning set leans toward citations of arXiv papers: of its references were matched by arXiv id.
How I checked
  • SciFact (Allen AI, CC BY-NC 2.0): expert-written claims derived from citation sentences, each paired with a cited abstract and an expert label. I sampled items labelled supports, labelled contradicts, and labelled not enough information.
  • arXiv: recent cs.LG and cs.CL papers. A parser extracted sentences that cite exactly one reference; references were matched to arXiv records. One real and one swapped item per paper.
  • Models were called through each provider's API with identical prompts and structured output. Costs are list prices applied to the tokens each call reported, counting reasoning tokens.
  • Verification was done by models, not people: agreement across all four models, then full-text reads by two models from different families.
  • Data preparation was spot-checked by a model: of extracted sentences compared with their LaTeX source, were faithful; of matched references, were the same work as the bibliography entry.
  • Context: fake references are already counted at scale (a 2026 audit of 2.5 million biomedical papers; Phantom References on top ML venues). Those checks ask whether a reference exists, not whether it supports the claim.
