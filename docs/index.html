Fake references are already being counted. Wrong citations are not.

Language models make up references, and audits now count them at scale: a 2026 audit of 2.5 million biomedical papers found fabricated references rising about six-fold in two years (summary), and Phantom References found them in roughly 1 in 20 accepted NeurIPS 2025 papers.

Those checks ask whether a reference exists. They cannot catch a real paper cited for something it never said. That needs a reader, and the question for anyone screening manuscripts is whether a model can be that reader: which one, how often it is right, and what it costs.

How we checked

  • SciFact (Allen AI, CC BY-NC 2.0): expert-written claims derived from citation sentences, each paired with a cited abstract and an expert label. We sampled claim–abstract items labelled supports, labelled contradicts, and labelled not enough information.
  • arXiv: recent cs.LG and cs.CL papers. A parser extracted sentences that cite exactly one reference; references were matched to arXiv records. One real and one swapped item per paper.
  • Models were called through each provider's API with identical prompts and structured output. Costs are list prices applied to the tokens each call reported, counting reasoning tokens.
  • Verification was done by models, not people: agreement across all models, then full-text reads by two models from different families.
  • Data preparation was spot-checked by a model as well: of extracted sentences compared with their LaTeX source, were faithful; of matched references, were the same work as the bibliography entry.

What this does not show

  • How common unsupported citations are. The sample was not designed to measure that.
  • Expert-level verification of the arXiv examples. They were checked by models reading full text.
  • That results transfer across fields. SciFact is biomedical; the arXiv set is machine learning. They are reported separately.
  • Performance on real mistakes. Swapped citations are easier to catch than the subtle errors authors actually make.
